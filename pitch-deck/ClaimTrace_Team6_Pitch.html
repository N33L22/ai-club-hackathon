CHECKPOINTS 2+3

Where the student is in control

① Approve the search plan and sources

② Resolve every flag on every claim

③ Sign off before export

Where answers are checked

Step 5: code checks existence, metadata and retraction status. Step 6: code re-checks the evidence sentence before any ‘Supported’ label appears.

Why the AI stays narrow

Scout only plans searches, Skeptic only challenges claims, Assistant only explains a source. None can approve a source, write a reference or edit the draft.

Architecture: small, modular and buildable

One web app, one AI provider and two free scholarly databases; the student’s data stays in their browser. AI has three narrow roles; every other check is plain code.

Web app

Next.js + React, hosted on Vercel

Library

Editor

Claim panel

Export

sources · labels · evidence

Orchestrator · browser workflow + Next.js API routes

In the browser: runs the 7 steps in order, enforces the 3 checkpoints, logs every action.

On the server: API routes call the AI and the databases, so the keys never reach the browser.

AI model

NVIDIA NIM API

Nemotron 3 Ultra in three roles: Scout, Skeptic, Assistant. JSON output, temperature 0.

Verifier

PLAIN CODE · NO AI

Fuzzy title and author match, evidence checks and retraction flag.

Scholarly APIs

OPENALEX + CROSSREF

Real records: DOIs, authors, abstracts, open-access links, retractions.

Storage

BROWSER · NO ACCOUNTS

Library, claims, evidence and audit log stay on the student’s device. No server database.

Data flow

1

Question → Scout (NIM) → search terms

2

Terms → OpenAlex → real records only

3

Student approves → library in their browser

4

Claim + source text → Verifier + Skeptic → label and evidence sentence

5

Built for day-of changes

The first version already runs end to end, so the 90 minutes go on adapting it. Each check is its own function: a new rule, source or style plugs in without a rewrite. AI or database down: ‘Unchecked’ or ‘Couldn’t check’, never a fake result.

04 · GUARDRAILS

Guardrails: six mechanisms that keep it honest

Code checks every citation against the databases, and ‘Supported’ needs the exact source sentence as proof. Tested on 20 labelled claims: 17–19 judged correctly across two runs, none wrongly marked ‘Supported’.

01

Retrieval-only references

The AI can’t write a citation. Each one needs a DOI or OpenAlex ID returned by the database.

02

Metadata must match

Title ≥90% similar, plus first author and year (±1). Near-misses become ‘Close match’ to compare and confirm.

03

Quote or it didn’t happen

‘Supported’ needs a whole source sentence that code finds in the text and that shares the claim’s key terms.

04

Scope check

Flags overreach such as ‘causes’, ‘all’, ‘proves’, ‘triples’ or the wrong population when the study shows less.

05

Honest ‘Not found’

‘Not found’ only when both databases answered. Database down: ‘Couldn’t check’. No text: ‘Unchecked’.

06

Retraction + export gate

Retracted papers turn red. Export stays locked until every red flag is fixed or knowingly overridden.

05 · ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS

Ethical risks, and how the design answers each one

Every risk is handled by a specific feature in the product, not by a promise.

RISK

WHY IT MATTERS

HOW CLAIMTRACE RESPONDS

Invented citations

AI writes convincing references that don’t exist.

Retrieval-only references: every citation comes from OpenAlex or Crossref, and its details must match the record.

Misrepresented research

A real paper is cited for something it never says.

The Skeptic shows the exact source sentence beside the claim and flags overreach; the student makes the call.

Plagiarism and patchwriting

Wording is copied too closely from the source.

Quotes need quote marks and a page number; sentences that reuse long runs of source wording are flagged to quote or rewrite.

Privacy

Drafts and research topics are personal.

Drafts stay in the student’s browser. Only the claim and source text go to the AI, never names or whole drafts. Students can delete one project or everything.

Biased or narrow sources

Databases over-represent some regions, languages and journals.

A source-mix view shows years, countries and open-access share. No single ‘credibility score’: fit is judged claim by claim.

Over-reliance on AI

Students accept AI output without thinking it through.

The AI never decides: three checkpoints, every flag needs a click, and ‘Supported’ means ‘sentence found’, not ‘this is true’.

06 · GOVERNANCE

Governance: the student stays the author

Clear accountability, automatic AI disclosure and named policies, so a supervisor can see exactly what the AI did and what the student decided.

Accountability

The student is the author of record and makes every final decision.

ClaimTrace gives evidence, not verdicts. Its known limits are shown in the app.

Every AI output and human decision is logged with time, model and action.

Team 6 owns the system’s behaviour and fixes reported errors.

AI disclosure

Inline labels separate AI reasoning from verified sources.

Every export includes an AI-use statement: steps that used AI, the model, the date and what the student changed.

Any demo output that is not a live AI call is labelled ‘Sample’.

Policies followed

SP Jain academic integrity and AI-use policy

APA 7, including APA guidance on citing generative AI

UNESCO guidance on generative AI in education and research (2023)

Data minimisation in line with GDPR principles

OpenAlex, Crossref and NVIDIA terms of use
